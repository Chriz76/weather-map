RapidWind documentation

RapidWind is a free, ad-free wind and weather map for kite, wing foil and windsurfing. It renders three regional rapid-update models – ICON-D2 RUC, AROME PI and HRRR – and turns any spot on the map into a point forecast with wind speed, gusts, wind direction and weather.

What RapidWind shows

Most consumer weather apps render global models with a 13–27 km grid and four runs per day. RapidWind renders regional rapid-update models instead: 2.2 km for Germany and Central Europe, 2.5 km for France and Western Europe, 3 km for the continental United States. A new model run arrives every hour, and the map is redrawn with it.

The wind field is drawn as a coloured overlay; a click or tap anywhere on the map opens the point forecast for that coordinate. The colour scale is tuned to the range that matters for kite and wing foiling, not to storm warning colours.

How to read the map

  • Model switch: the logo row at the top toggles between ICON-D2 RUC, AROME PI and HRRR. Each model covers a different region.
  • Timeline: step through the forecast hours. Map and point forecast always follow the selected hour.
  • Point forecast: click or tap a spot on the map to get wind, gusts, direction and the WMO weather code for that coordinate.
  • Share: the share control copies a link to the current view so the same location can be reopened later or sent to someone else.
  • GPS: centres the map on your own position, useful when you are at the spot and want to compare forecast and reality.
  • Wind display: switches how the wind field is drawn – as the coloured surface or with direction arrows.

The three models

Resolution, run cadence, output interval and horizon of what the map renders.
Model Region Grid Runs Steps on the map Map horizon
ICON-D2 RUC Germany and Central Europe 2.2 km hourly 60 min 24 h
AROME PI France and Western Europe 2.5 km base, 1.3 km gusts hourly 15 min for 6 h, then hourly 22 h
HRRR Continental United States 3 km hourly 15 min for 6 h, then hourly 18 h

ICON-D2 RUC is requested at its native hourly step: the DWD publishes neither 10 m wind nor weather codes below one hour for the RUC, so a 15-minute series would only interpolate the hourly field. AROME PI and HRRR do publish 15-minute steps; the map shows them for the first six hours and switches to hourly values after that.

The models themselves reach further than the map. An ICON-D2 RUC run covers 27 h, AROME France continues beyond AROME PI, and HRRR runs go out to 48 h. The map and the timeline end where the data pipeline stops publishing – 24 h, 22 h and 18 h respectively. Point forecasts may extend a little further, because they come from Open-Meteo instead of the pipeline.

Why resolution and update cycle matter

Micro-climatic shifts, thermal winds, sea breeze and localized frontal systems near lakes or coastal structures are lost in grids coarser than about 10 km. A 2–3 km grid resolves these thermodynamic anomalies, and the 1.3 km gust field of AROME PI resolves them one step further.

The update cycle decides whether the forecast is still usable when the wind changes. Global models are refreshed every six hours; the models shown here get a new run every hour, so a front that arrives late still shows up in the near-term tactical window – the part of the day that decides whether a session happens.

Data sources and attribution

None of the forecast data is measured by RapidWind itself. It is fetched from the following sources, cropped to the map area and rendered; the attribution below is required by their terms of use.

  • ICON-D2 RUC: Deutscher Wetterdienst (DWD), DWD Open Data. GRIB2 fields are cropped and serialized by the RapidWind data pipeline.
  • AROME PI: Météo-France model data, delivered through Open-Meteo.
  • HRRR: NOAA HRRR model data; wind tiles from the RapidWind data pipeline, point forecasts and weather codes through Open-Meteo.
  • Point forecasts and weather codes: Open-Meteo for all three providers, including the WMO weather codes.
  • Basemap: map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, tiles © CARTO. The attribution is also shown in the map corner of the app.
  • Weather icons: Weather Icons by Erik Flowers.

Limits and disclaimer

Always check the local weather at your spot. A forecast can be wrong, and a model cannot see the tree line, the building or the headland in front of you.
  • The models are regional. Outside their coverage area the map has no data; the app does not fall back to a global model.
  • A point forecast is the value of the grid cell containing your coordinate, not a measurement at your position.
  • Rapid updates sharpen the near term, they do not extend it. Beyond the model horizon you need a second source.
  • This project is under active development; behaviour and data coverage can change.

Privacy

No account, no marketing cookies, no tracking across sites, and none of your personal data is stored by RapidWind. A minimal amount of processing happens in the background to deliver the site safely and quickly, on the basis of legitimate interest (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR):

  • Hosting: the site is served as a static build through Cloudflare Pages (Cloudflare Inc., 101 Townsend St, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA). Server logs contain the IP address, browser type and time of access, which is technically required to deliver the page and to block attacks.
  • Content delivery: Cloudflare acts as CDN and security layer in front of the origin. Cloudflare may set technically necessary cookies for security purposes; they do not create user profiles.
  • Error tracking: Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., San Francisco, USA) receives anonymized error reports without tracking cookies and with IP addresses removed.
  • Analytics: Umami, operated without cookies, without persistent identifiers and without cross-site tracking. All values are aggregated and anonymized.

Forecast data itself is fetched from DWD, Open-Meteo and the RapidWind data pipeline; no user data is sent to these sources.
